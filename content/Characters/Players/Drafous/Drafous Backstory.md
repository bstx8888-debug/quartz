## The First Correction  
It took time to learn the systems of this world. Currency. Trade. Magic.  
The first thing I built was simple: a stabilized heating core. Small. Durable. Self-sustaining.  
I sold it to a coastal settlement that had been paying a local wizard for seasonal warming enchantments.  While paid more than what they would pay in a season, it would save them money in the long run.
They stopped paying him.  
The device worked.  
It did not degrade. It did not require maintenance. It did not require permission.  
They did not go back.  
That was the first lesson of Zerith I understood:  
<p style="color:#cc3333; font-weight:bold; margin-top:-20px;">&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The system persists because no one has replaced it.  </p>
<div style="page-break-before: always;"></div>

## The Second Correction  
Months later, I refined the design. Reduced cost. Increased output. Standardized the construction.  
I produced three more.  
Then ten.  Then twenty.  
The local wizard attempted to intervene—claimed the work was unauthorized, unstable, dangerous.  
It was not.  
When his services were no longer purchased, he left.  
No one replaced him.  
Nothing failed.  
That was the second lesson of Zerith I understood:  
<p style="color:#cc3333; font-weight:bold; margin-top:-20px;">&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Control is maintained through dependency.  </p>

## The Third Correction: Meeting [[Korrin Vhalgrin]]  
I met Korrin two years later.  
He approached me, not as a client, but as a critic.  
He had acquired one of my early designs and disassembled it.  
He informed me, in direct terms, that my work was:  
- Inefficiently assembled  
- Difficult to reproduce at scale  
- Structurally inconsistent  

He was correct.  
Korrin Vhalgrin is a dwarf of considerable skill. Where I design systems, he understands structure. Where I see function, he sees failure points.  
We began working together immediately.  
He refined my designs:  
- Standardized components  
- Reinforced stress points  
- Reduced material waste  
- Increased production speed

The tenth unit he helped produce was superior to the first twenty I had made alone.  
That was the third lesson of Zerith I understood:  
<p style="color:#cc3333; font-weight:bold; margin-top:-20px;">&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; If something can be improved, it will be.</p>

## The Formation of the Technomancers  
What began as individual construction became coordinated production.  
I handled design and iteration; Korrin handled fabrication and process
We trained others. Not wizards in the traditional sense—though many had magical training—but individuals willing to build, test, and refine.  

Four years ago, we formalized the operation.  
We called it the [[Technomancers]].  
Not a guild of theory.  
A guild of application.  
## Growth  
The model scaled faster than anticipated.  
Standardized designs allowed for:  
- Rapid training of new craftsmen  
- Consistent production across multiple workshops  
- Reduced reliance on specialized casters  

Demand increased.  Not because we persuaded anyone.  Because the work functioned.  We now number approximately sixty members.
The **Tenfold Circuit** serve as primary leads across disciplines:

## Position  
Wizard factions continue to operate.  
Their services remain available.  
But in the regions where our work spreads, they are no longer necessary.  
This has not gone unnoticed.  
## [[Zanzer Tem]]  
Recently, Korrin uncovered a lead on a wizard named [[Zanzer Tem]].  
His research into item enchantment suggested a level of efficiency and scalability approaching my own work.  
I intended to meet him.  
To evaluate his methods.  
To determine whether he could be integrated into the [[Technomancers]].  
Or if he would become competition.  
I remember setting out to meet him.  
## The Fourth Correction  
I do not remember arriving.  
I woke in a [[Zanzer Tem's Dungeon|jail cell]].
This was the fourth lesson of Zerith I understood:


<p style="color:#cc3333; font-weight:bold; margin-top:-20px;">&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Disrupting the system can have unforeseen consequences </p>

# Drafous Overdrive

Drafous is inherently a cool blue.  This denotes his calm, planning, precise nature. in times of heightened states, such as combat, or when he must excel beyond his normal form, his tone will start to shift.  The more he taps into his greater power, the stronger the glow, and his tone will start to take on a red hue. Starting as a red glow within his core, the light will spread, overtaking the blue, eventually shining through his whole body.
